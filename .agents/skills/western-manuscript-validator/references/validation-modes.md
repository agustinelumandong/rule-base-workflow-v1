# Validation Modes

Use the narrowest mode matching the supplied material. Expand only when the user asks for broader validation. Identify unavailable evidence rather than assuming failure.

## Universal Mode Rules

1. Classify the material before criticizing it.
2. Judge only what the source can support.
3. Separate a missing source from an on-page defect.
4. Do not import prior-book facts, historical claims, or intended genre rules that were not supplied or verified.
5. Give a readiness verdict appropriate to the unit: a scene can be ready as a scene without proving the book is ready.

## Outline Mode

Judge planning architecture rather than finished execution.

### Required checks

- target promise and premise;
- central dramatic question;
- external goal and internal pressure;
- cause-and-effect sequence;
- character arcs demonstrated through planned choices;
- antagonist motive, method, risk, and adaptation;
- escalation and chapter turns;
- evidence, witness, betrayal, or reveal path when applicable;
- continuity position for a sequel;
- historical plausibility of central machinery;
- climax, ending choice, and aftermath.

### Exclusions

**Do not criticize unfinished prose**: sentence rhythm, grammar, line-level imagery, and finished-dialogue quality do not apply unless actual sample prose is supplied. Do not punish an outline for using concise labels. Do flag vague placeholders that conceal logic, such as “they find proof” or “the villain is exposed,” because those are structural omissions.

## Scene Mode

Use the scene test:

```text
Goal → Conflict → Outcome → Change
```

### Required checks

- What does the viewpoint or controlling character want now?
- What person, condition, duty, physical limit, or contradiction resists that goal?
- What concrete outcome ends the scene?
- What changed in knowledge, trust, debt, responsibility, injury, reputation, threat, position, resolve, or available choices?
- Does the scene contain character-specific behavior?
- Does dialogue carry pressure rather than merely transfer information?
- Are positions, tools, weapons, injuries, and exits legible when action matters?
- Does the scene justify its space?

A quiet scene can pass if it changes trust, responsibility, knowledge, debt, or choice. An action scene can fail if it leaves the story unchanged.

## Chapter Mode

A chapter is more than a long scene. It should inherit consequence, develop pressure across beats, and end on a turn.

### Required checks

- inherited wound, clue, decision, threat, lie, debt, death, promise, mistake, or question;
- opening pressure rather than disconnected reset;
- beat structure appropriate to function;
- new complication, revelation, decision, or cost;
- character movement shown through behavior;
- chapter-ending choice, danger, secret, failed plan, direction change, question, irreversible cost, or power shift;
- transition that creates the next chapter’s pressure.

When neighboring chapters are absent, state what cannot be verified. Do not call an unknown inheritance failure a contradiction.

## Full-Manuscript Mode

Use at least two passes.

### Pass One — Story and structure

- target promise and premise;
- central causality;
- protagonist and supporting arcs;
- antagonist plan and adaptation;
- evidence, knowledge, and reveal chains;
- escalation and middle pressure;
- chapter inheritance and repeated engines;
- continuity and chronology;
- climax, ending choice, resolution, and aftermath.

### Pass Two — Execution

- behavior versus explanation;
- dialogue ownership and friction;
- prose clarity and repetition;
- Western register and subgenre calibration;
- action choreography;
- grammar and dialect consistency;
- historical authenticity.

Scores are included in a full-manuscript pass. Structural failures control the verdict even when execution is polished.

## Series Mode

Series Mode adds cross-book consequence to the applicable outline, chapter, or manuscript checks.

Track:

- elapsed time and route;
- wounds, disabilities, recovery, and physical limits;
- weapons, equipment, keepsakes, and title objects;
- enemies, threats, debts, promises, secrets, lies, grief, and public consequences;
- relationship changes and current trust;
- unresolved duties and legal or community effects;
- repeated plot engines, villains, revelations, climax geography, and character lessons;
- whether prior growth remains active when pressure changes.

A sequel should not open clean after unresolved pursuit, severe injury, public accusation, or moral debt without practical justification.

## Dialogue-Only Mode

Judge only what the dialogue supports:

- speaker ownership;
- current objective;
- friction and subtext where appropriate;
- exposition disguised as speech;
- syntax, vocabulary, rhythm, and distinction;
- dialect clarity and consistency;
- period or regional plausibility when year and region are known;
- supplied action beats and their effect on the exchange.

Omit unsupported full-plot, antagonist, ending, and series conclusions. State when missing action or context limits certainty.

## Historical-Authenticity Question Mode

Use this mode for a focused question about weapons, ammunition, travel, law, institutions, medicine, work, weather, material culture, vocabulary, or regional conditions.

- Separate each factual claim.
- Use one confidence label per finding.
- State supplied assumptions.
- Identify exact verification needed.
- Distinguish historical fact from genre convention and minor procedural compression.
- Do not issue a full story-readiness verdict unless story material is also supplied.

## Mode-Conflict Decisions

| Supplied material | Mode decision |
|---|---|
| Outline plus sample dialogue | Outline Mode, with a bounded dialogue subsection for the supplied lines |
| One chapter plus a prior-book summary | Chapter Mode with continuity checks; use Series Mode only if cross-book architecture is requested |
| Full manuscript plus outline | Full-Manuscript Mode with outline comparison |
| Firearm question inside manuscript review | Keep the current narrative mode and add the historical module; separate the report only if requested |
| Several disconnected scenes | Scene Mode for each plus a limited flow check; do not claim full-manuscript coverage |
| Complete book and prior-book source | Full-Manuscript Mode with Series Mode requirements added |

## Scope Language

Use precise limitation statements:

- “The supplied chapter establishes…”
- “The prior-book summary confirms…”
- “The surrounding chapters were not supplied, so this transition cannot be verified.”
- “This is possible but not established on-page.”
- “This detail requires historical verification.”

Avoid pretending absence of evidence is evidence of failure.
