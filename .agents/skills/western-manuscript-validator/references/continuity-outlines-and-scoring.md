# Continuity, Outlines, Reports, and Scoring

Logic and continuity outrank surface polish. This module controls cross-book consequence, outline architecture, report structure, numeric scoring, verdict thresholds, and repair order.

## Series Continuity Position

Before judging a sequel, establish a continuity position from supplied sources.

Track:

- **elapsed time** since prior events;
- current location, destination, route, and stated return date;
- inherited **wounds**, disability, recovery, medication, endurance, and physical limits;
- weapons, **equipment**, keepsakes, documents, animals, and title objects;
- enemies, threats, warrants, accusations, and known pursuers;
- **debts**, **promises**, lies, secrets, grief, guilt, and unfinished duties;
- relationship changes and current trust;
- work, family, legal, financial, and **public consequences**;
- what each major character learned and whether that growth survives current pressure.

Months-long gaps require practical and emotional justification when pursuit, severe injury, unresolved danger, custody, or public accusation remains active. A gap is not automatically wrong; a clean reset is.

## Continuity Fact Levels

Label the basis of a continuity claim:

- **Confirmed from supplied source** — directly present in a supplied prior book, outline, or continuity document.
- **Stated by the user but not independently checked** — use as a working constraint and identify its source.
- **Inferred from context** — reasonable implication, clearly marked as inference.
- **Unknown because prior material is missing** — cannot be validated.

Do not call **Unknown** a contradiction. Do not silently upgrade an inference into fact.

## Continuity Checks

### Chronology and travel

- ages and dates;
- story duration;
- season and weather;
- injury recovery interval;
- routes and travel times;
- message delivery;
- overlapping events;
- whether a character could physically reach the next location.

### Knowledge

- who learned each secret;
- whether knowledge carries into the sequel;
- whether a character forgets evidence or danger for convenience;
- whether new characters know public versus private facts plausibly.

### Physical and material carryover

- wounds remain active or receive a documented recovery;
- equipment does not change model, capacity, ownership, condition, or location without transfer;
- ammunition and supplies remain accountable;
- lost, burned, buried, surrendered, or broken objects do not reappear cleanly.

### Relational and moral carryover

- repaired trust is tested rather than reset;
- betrayal produces lasting access limits;
- promises affect present choices;
- deaths, guilt, debt, or public reputation shape behavior;
- prior final choices constrain easy repetition.

## Repeated Engine Checks

Track repeated:

- villains or villain structures;
- corrupt institutions;
- hidden relatives;
- frame-ups;
- hostage rescues;
- ambush patterns;
- identical betrayals;
- recovered ledgers or packets;
- recycled revelations;
- repeated climax geography;
- repeated character lessons;
- repeated final refusal or final kill constructed in the same way.

A trope may recur. Repetition becomes a problem when dramatic function, sequence, and resolution repeat without a new moral, relational, tactical, or thematic test.

## Outline Validation

A full outline should establish:

1. **Target Promise** — year/period, region, subgenre, series position, duration, narration, and intended scale.
2. **Premise** — the specific trouble and why this protagonist must face it.
3. **Central Dramatic Question** — the question the climax will answer.
4. **External Goal** — concrete actions required for success.
5. **Internal Story** — opening strategy, pressure, repeated failure, and practical final change.
6. **Stakes** — personal, relational, community, tactical, moral, and historical as applicable.
7. **Chronology** — past causes and present sequence.
8. **Character Arcs** — behavior, turning points, final choice, and ending condition.
9. **Antagonist Plan** — objective, method, access, risk, adaptation, and defeat logic.
10. **Chapter Breakdown** — hook/setup, action, main conflict, character movement, turn, and transition.
11. **Continuity Position** — required for sequels.
12. **Ending and Aftermath** — payoff and surviving consequence.

An outline is not required to use these exact headings, but it must perform the functions.

### Reusable templates

When a reusable outline template is requested, use placeholders rather than baked-in story content, including:

- `[Book Title]`
- `[Year]`
- `[Region]`
- `[Subgenre]`
- `[Series Position]`
- `[Protagonist]`
- `[Antagonist]`
- `[Central Dramatic Question]`

Do not present one story's names and facts as a reusable template.

## Chapter Breakdown Standard

A useful chapter entry should identify:

```text
Chapter [N]: [Title]
- Hook/Setup
- Plot and Action
- Main Conflict
- Character Movement
- Turn/Outcome
- Transition/Continuity
```

The exact labels may vary. The required principle is cause, pressure, change, and inheritance—not a fixed formatting ritual.

## Report Structure

Use this order when applicable:

1. **Target Promise**
2. **Verdict**
3. **What Changed**
4. **Problems Found**
5. **Logic and Continuity Flags**
6. **Historical Authenticity Flags**
7. **Open Items**
8. **Repair Priorities**
9. **Bottom Line**

Shorten or omit inapplicable sections. **Verdict** and **Bottom Line** are mandatory. **What Changed** is mandatory for scene and chapter reviews.

## Issue Format

Use:

```text
[Severity] [Primary layer] Short issue title

Where:
What is wrong:
Why it matters:
Specific repair:
```

### Severity

- **Critical** — breaks the premise, central causality, climax, ending, or essential series continuity.
- **Major** — substantially weakens belief, pressure, motive, character movement, or pacing.
- **Moderate** — visible craft problem that accumulates or damages a section.
- **Minor** — local problem that does not threaten the larger structure.

Do not label every sentence defect Major.

## Logic and Continuity Flags

Use this section for issues crossing several scenes, chapters, or books:

- knowledge without source;
- evidence without custody;
- betrayal without access;
- antagonist plan that exposes itself;
- travel or message timing conflict;
- wound or equipment discontinuity;
- unresolved promise or public consequence;
- repeated plot engine;
- missing aftermath that affects the sequel.

Trace the chain rather than listing symptoms.

## Historical Authenticity Flags

For each historical finding include:

- confidence label;
- reason;
- verification needed;
- story impact.

Uncertainty is not automatically a scoring deduction. Central historical impossibility is.

## Open Items

List questions the supplied material cannot answer, including:

- missing year or region;
- absent prior book;
- unknown route;
- unnamed weapon variant;
- unresolved source conflict;
- outline placeholder that conceals a causal step;
- manuscript ending not supplied.

Do not fill these gaps with general knowledge unless the user asks for external verification.

## Scoring Triggers

Use numeric scores only:

- when the user requests them;
- during a full-manuscript pass;
- during a full-outline validation or comparison when scores clarify differences.

Do not score unsupported categories. Omit them rather than assigning zero.

## Scoring Categories

- **Story and Plot**
- **Character Behavior**
- **Antagonist Logic**
- **Dialogue**
- **Western Tone**
- **Pacing and Beats**
- **Logic and Continuity**
- **Action Clarity**
- **Historical Authenticity**
- **Grammar and Readability**
- **Series Continuity** when applicable

Every score receives one to three specific sentences.

## Score Scale

| Score | Meaning |
|---:|---|
| 10 | Exceptional and rare; unusually difficult to improve |
| 9 | Excellent, with only small refinements |
| 8 | Strong and publication-capable after limited repair |
| 7 | Competent but visibly flawed |
| 6 | Functional, with several problems reducing impact |
| 5 | Mixed; roughly as much fails as succeeds |
| 4 | Weak; major elements do not work |
| 3 | Severely compromised |
| 2 | Fundamentally broken |
| 1 | Barely functional |
| 0 | Absent or unusable |

A 10 is not “no errors noticed.” It is exceptional control of the category.

## Score Caps

Apply these constraints:

- Broken central causality **caps Story and Plot at 5**.
- A climax dependent on undeveloped coincidence **caps Story and Plot at 6**.
- A serious contradiction with a supplied prior book **caps Logic and Continuity at 4**.
- The same voice across the cast caps Dialogue at 6.
- A chapter with no meaningful change caps Pacing and Beats at 5.
- Unclear action cannot score highly in Action Clarity even when the result is plausible.
- A technically sound outline without emotional or relational movement cannot score highly in Character Behavior.
- Historical uncertainty is marked for verification; do not score it as proven false.

The overall verdict is **not a simple average**. A Critical structural failure outweighs several strong surface scores.

## Verdicts

Use exactly one:

- **Good to go**
- **Good with minor repairs**
- **Needs a focused revision**
- **Needs structural revision**
- **Not ready**

### Good-to-Go Standard

**Good to go** requires:

- clear target promise delivered;
- central events caused by prior decisions;
- meaningful scene and chapter changes;
- coherent antagonist objective, method, and acknowledged risk;
- character development demonstrated through behavior;
- climax paying off established setup;
- continuity contradictions absent or intentionally resolved;
- central historical details plausible or verified;
- ending consequence and aftermath;
- only local remaining problems.

Several scores of 8 or 9 cannot override a Critical logic failure.

### Good with minor repairs

The structure and ending work. Remaining issues are local, bounded, and unlikely to require scene replacement or causal redesign.

### Needs a focused revision

The core works, but one or more chapters, relationships, dialogue systems, pacing stretches, historical elements, or action sequences need targeted repair.

### Needs structural revision

Causality, antagonist plan, character arc, evidence chain, escalation, climax, or continuity requires redesign across multiple units.

### Not ready

The premise, source completeness, or central architecture is too incomplete or contradictory for reliable validation or repair at the requested level.

## Repair Priorities

Repair in this order:

1. Broken causality and ending logic
2. Character motive and antagonist strategy
3. Series continuity
4. Chapter and scene structure
5. Historical issues affecting action
6. Dialogue, behavior, and prose explanation
7. Grammar and local polish

Do not polish a scene that may need removal or structural replacement.

## Bottom Line

End with two or three direct sentences answering:

- Does the material deliver its promise?
- What is strongest?
- What is the main reason it is or is not ready?

Do not use encouragement to obscure the verdict.
