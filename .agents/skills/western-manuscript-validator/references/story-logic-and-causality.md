# Story Logic and Causality

Story logic asks whether each important development follows from established motive, knowledge, access, ability, and consequence. A narrator's confidence is not evidence that a plan works.

## Base Causality Trace

Use this sequence for every major event:

```text
Motive
→ Decision
→ Action
→ Observable consequence
→ Response
→ New pressure
```

A missing link is a **Story** problem unless the narrative intentionally withholds it and later supplies a credible answer. Distinguish:

- **Established** — the source demonstrates the link.
- **Possible but not established** — the link could work, but the source has not earned it.
- **Contradicted** — supplied facts make the link fail.
- **Unknown** — necessary source material is absent.

## Knowledge and Access Trace

Use this sequence for secrets, betrayal, ambush, planted evidence, private routes, and hidden preparations:

```text
Who knew?
→ How did they know?
→ What access did they possess?
→ What did they do?
→ What trace did the action leave?
→ Who could discover it?
→ Why was it not discovered earlier?
```

Ask separately:

- Was the information spoken, written, observed, inferred, stolen, or leaked?
- Did the character have time and physical access?
- Did the source establish the relevant skill or tool?
- Would the act create witnesses, tracks, missing objects, altered schedules, wounds, noise, smoke, or paperwork?
- Why does the character trust the information?
- What happens if the information is incomplete or false?

Motive never substitutes for access. Access never substitutes for motive.

## Physical Possibility

Trace bodies, objects, time, and terrain.

### Check

- travel distance, pace, weather, rest, water, remounts, and route;
- line of sight and firing angle;
- weapon reach, loading state, recoil, capacity, and available hand;
- injury limits during lifting, mounting, climbing, grappling, and sustained aim;
- body position before and after each action;
- object possession and transfer;
- door, window, ladder, wagon, horse, and cover positions;
- time required for preparation, cleanup, and escape.

A sequence may be **possible but not established** rather than impossible. State which missing detail would make it legible.

## Evidence Chain

For physical, documentary, or testimonial evidence, trace:

```text
Creation
→ survival
→ custody
→ discovery
→ interpretation
→ corroboration or contradiction
→ consequence
```

### Test

- Who created or left the evidence?
- Was it intentional, accidental, forged, moved, contaminated, burned, rained on, trampled, or handled?
- Why did it survive chaos?
- Who controlled it between discovery and accusation?
- Could another explanation fit?
- Does the discoverer possess the knowledge to interpret it?
- Does independent evidence support it?
- Would an antagonist anticipate or manipulate its meaning?

A document does not solve a plot merely because it contains the answer. Its origin, custody, meaning, and credibility still require proof.

## Antagonist Plan

Test the antagonist as rigorously as the protagonist.

### Objective

- What specific outcome does the antagonist want?
- What does success look like today, not only in abstract terms?
- What emotional, practical, or reputational need drives the method?

### Method

- Why does this action advance the objective?
- What resources, allies, authority, money, horses, weapons, information, or local knowledge are available?
- What information is missing?
- What simpler alternative exists, and why is it rejected?

### Risk

Test both exposure risk and **self-harm risk**.

- How could the plan expose the antagonist?
- How could it harm their own interests or allies?
- What witness, record, body, weapon, route, or planted object could point backward?
- Does the antagonist understand that risk?
- What contingency exists if the first inference fails?

### Adaptation

- Does the antagonist learn from failure?
- Does pressure increase, change direction, or narrow choices?
- Or do repeated acts merely supply cleaner clues?
- Does each action impose real cost, delay, fear, division, injury, lost evidence, or tactical disadvantage?

### Defeat

The defeat should follow from:

```text
Established weakness or blind spot
+ protagonist/supporting-character action
+ accumulated consequence
```

Do not accept sudden stupidity, an unused obvious escape, or a harmless final attack merely to permit victory.

## Specialized Traces

### Frame-up

```text
Motive → access → planted evidence → false witness path → discovery → accusation → contradiction risk → contingency
```

Check:

- why this target is believable;
- how the framer obtained the object or imitation;
- what witness sees and what they only infer;
- who discovers the evidence;
- why the accusation forms before contradictions are checked;
- how the framer prevents the evidence from pointing back;
- what happens when one element fails.

### Betrayal

```text
Relationship and grievance → recruitment or decision → knowledge/access → leak or action → benefit → trace → response
```

Establish betrayal before the plot depends on it. A later revelation cannot retroactively grant access that never existed.

### Ambush

```text
Target route → source of route knowledge → chosen ground → setup time → concealment → trigger → escape/withdrawal → aftermath
```

Test why the target enters the ground, why scouts miss it, who controls timing, and what tracks or spent ammunition remain.

### Hidden History

```text
Past event → surviving participants/evidence → reason for silence → present trigger → discovery path → changed meaning → consequence
```

The reason the truth stayed hidden must be at least as strong as the pressure now revealing it.

### Mystery Reveal

```text
Question → clue sequence → false inference → contradiction → tested theory → reveal → proof → consequence
```

The reveal must reorganize earlier facts, not introduce the necessary facts for the first time.

### Sabotage

```text
Objective → vulnerable system → access → act → intended cost → misleading trace or concealment → discovery risk → next adaptation
```

Repeated sabotage that causes no cost and identifies the saboteur is not escalation.

### Forced Showdown

```text
Why the antagonist needs this ground/person/weapon → pressure that forces attendance → controlled positions → intended no-win choice → protagonist's alternative → immediate threat → consequence
```

Place, ritual, or inherited weapon should matter causally, not merely symbolically.

## Coincidence

Coincidence may:

- begin trouble;
- complicate trouble;
- reveal a possibility;
- create an obstacle that characters must answer.

Coincidence should not repeatedly:

- deliver decisive evidence;
- place the exact missing witness at the climax;
- save a character from an earned consequence;
- solve the central conflict.

Distinguish coincidence from convergence established through route, schedule, message, pursuit, employment, family tie, habit, or deliberate character choice.

## Setup and Payoff

Before the climax uses a tool, witness, skill, injury, route, object, ally, weakness, or moral claim, establish it clearly enough that the payoff feels inevitable in retrospect.

### Payoff test

- Was the element introduced before it became necessary?
- Was it used or tested earlier?
- Did its limit remain active?
- Did another character contribute agency rather than becoming a prop?
- Did the payoff create cost or consequence?

## Ending Causality

The ending must answer the central dramatic question through established choices.

Check:

- The climax grows from earlier decisions.
- The antagonist's defeat follows from strategy, weakness, and resistance.
- The protagonist's final choice differs meaningfully from the opening flawed strategy.
- Supporting characters retain useful agency.
- Violence answers an immediate threat rather than replacing moral judgment.
- Evidence, testimony, custody, or public consequence survives plausibly.
- Aftermath names wounds, deaths, debts, trust changes, reputation, and next obligations.

An abrupt stop after the last shot is not sufficient aftermath when the story promised moral, legal, family, or community consequence.

## Concrete Repair Patterns

Use repairs such as:

- Establish access in an earlier scene through work duty, overheard information, stolen notes, or observed preparation.
- Change who discovers evidence so discovery follows established position and skill.
- Add a contradiction the antagonist must anticipate and manage.
- Make sabotage impose a real cost before it reveals information.
- Put a witness on an established route, message chain, relationship, or pursuit rather than relying on chance.
- Preserve evidence through named custody, copies, witnesses, or physical separation rather than author convenience.
- Give the antagonist a reason not to use the simpler alternative: reputation, limited authority, need for deniability, personal obsession, unavailable resources, or risk to another objective.
- Move the reveal earlier if later chapters require characters to act on it.
- Separate what a witness observed from what the witness inferred.
- Let a failed plan force adaptation rather than repetition.

Do not repair a logic gap by adding narration that merely declares the event reasonable.
